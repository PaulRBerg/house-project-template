## Passive House as inspiration

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Passive House is a voluntary PHI framework with its
  own energy balance, comfort and testing criteria. This project uses those criteria only as design benchmarks and does
  not pursue certification (`SITE-08`). Source:
  [PHI building criteria](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download).

  Caveat: a benchmark met on paper is not a compliance result, a permit or a metered outcome.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Prove any benchmark and the nZEB legal requirement each
  with its own boundary and calculation method.

  Caveat: apparent agreement between headline metrics does not establish equivalence.

### Principles

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: General principle.** PHI identifies five linked principles: very good thermal
  insulation, highly energy-efficient windows, controlled mechanical ventilation with heat recovery, thermal-bridge-
  reduced construction, and airtightness. Source:
  [PHI building certification](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/).

  Caveat: these principles do
  not prescribe a universal wall, window, ventilation unit, or system for Romania. The climate, geometry, use, details,
  and PHPP balance control the project solution.

### Benchmark values (PHI v10c)

The values below come from PHI _Criteria for Buildings_ version 10c (2023-01-20, valid with PHPP 10). They are reference
benchmarks the household may compare designs against, not targets it must certify. The
[criteria PDF](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download) is the canonical source.

PHI v10c/PHPP 10, 2023-01-20.                                                                        | These are alternative benchmark paths, not a promise of annual metered heat or utility cost. Warm-up after setbacks is outside the stated load boundary.                                                 |

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** When comparing against PHI criteria, state which
  criteria version and PHPP release the numbers come from. Source:
  [PHI v10c criteria](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download).

  Caveat: "current criteria" and the criteria behind an older published result can legitimately differ.

### Climate inputs

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** For any energy model, use a climate data set
  representative of the site and record its source and version. A PHI-approved set is one option for benchmark
  comparison, not a requirement.

  Caveat: an unverified online climate export is not design evidence.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** For the project's locality (`<project location>`, set once the parcel is known), obtain current quality-controlled
  hourly weather for load, overheating, dehumidification, and resilience studies, plus exact coordinates/elevation,
  terrain and exposure, site horizon, adjacent and future shading, façade geometry, ground properties, groundwater, and
  intended occupancy/operation. Supporting source:
  [PHI climate-data guidance](https://passipedia.org/planning/climate_data_tool).

  Caveat: a PHPP benchmark comparison
  and an hourly simulation are separate studies whose scenario, period and model must each be documented.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Treat every envelope or system conclusion for the project's locality as an
  inference until those inputs and the actual site, program, construction, and use assumptions exist.

  Caveat: locality name
  alone cannot establish orientation, insulation, glazing, shading, ground-loop, moisture, or equipment decisions.

## Envelope and climate

Researched/accessed 2026-08-10. Use this as a decision and verification checklist, not as a project specification.

### Form and solar control

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Test compactness, orientation, room/use distribution,
  daylight, and solar access together in the energy and summer-comfort models. Supporting source:
  [PHI planning overview](https://passipedia.org/planning).

  Caveat: compactness can reduce envelope loss but does not
  override the site, program, daylight, accessibility, fire, or architectural constraints.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Size glazing and fixed/movable shading per façade using
  actual horizon, reveal, overhang, vegetation, nearby-building, glass, frame, and control assumptions. Supporting
  source:
  [PHI summer-comfort risk guidance](https://passipedia.org/planning/summer_comfort/risk_analysis_robust_planning).

  Caveat: a south-façade rule cannot be copied to east, west, north, or roof glazing, and operable shading depends on
  controls and occupant use.

### Thermal and airtight continuity

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: General principle.** Draw one continuous insulation layer around every heated
  volume section and resolve all transitions, penetrations, supports, and service zones. Source:
  [PHI's five principles](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/).

  Caveat: nominal insulation
  thickness does not prove installed continuity, effective U-value, moisture safety, or durability.

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Calculate material junctions with suitable
  two-dimensional models and point/support conditions with three-dimensional models. Derive heat flow and minimum
  surface temperatures under documented boundaries. Source: [ISO 10211:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/65710.html),
  confirmed current in 2022.

  Caveat: its stated steady-property assumptions and selected boundary conditions may not
  capture transient moisture, workmanship, or every metal fixing.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Name one primary continuous airtight layer, show it on
  all sections, and detail each material change, window/door junction, roof/wall/slab transition, penetration, and
  temporary construction state. Supporting source:
  [PHI certification guidance](https://passipedia.org/certification/certified_passive_houses).

  Caveat: “airtight
  material” is not an airtight assembly. Access, substrate preparation, compatible connections, sequencing, and
  inspection matter.

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** While leaks remain accessible, run a
  construction-stage diagnostic blower-door test. After the envelope and penetrations are complete, run a final test.
  Record test boundary, building preparation, pressurization and depressurization, environmental conditions, volume,
  leakage search, result, and corrections. Source: [ISO 9972:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/55718.html), reviewed
  and confirmed in 2026, and [PHI v10c criteria](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download).

  Caveat: ISO 9972
  measures the tested building boundary, not individual components, and changing preparation or volume invalidates
  casual comparison. The household budgets one diagnostic blower-door test with leak identification (`SITE-08`).

### Windows and junctions

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Evaluate the installed whole window: actual frame
  fractions, glazing, spacers, opening type, installation position, jamb/head/sill thermal bridges, airtight/watertight
  connections, shading hardware, drainage, and fixings. Source:
  [PHI window-installation guidance](https://passipedia.org/planning/thermal_protection/windows/window_installation).

  Caveat: centre-of-glass Ug or catalogue-frame values alone do not establish installed Uw, surface temperature, solar
  gain, air/water performance, or workmanship.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Verify each distinct junction and installation variant
  against the actual drawings and product geometry rather than substituting a visually similar catalogue detail.
  Supporting source:
  [PHI thermal-bridge calculation guidance](https://passipedia.org/basics/building_physics_-_basics/thermal_bridges/tbcalculation/thermal_bridges_of_transparent_components).

  Caveat: manufacturer examples remain conditional on matching dimensions, materials, conductivities, fixings, and
  installation position.

### Heat, rain, and moisture

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Test summer overheating and dehumidification with current
  weather scenarios, façade-specific solar gains, shading availability and failure, internal loads, ventilation modes,
  night-purge feasibility, thermal mass, and active-system capacity. Supporting source:
  [PHI summer-comfort risk guidance](https://passipedia.org/planning/summer_comfort/risk_analysis_robust_planning).

  Caveat: a single annual result can conceal peak rooms, heat waves, humidity, power loss, closed windows, or failed
  shading.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: General principle.** Establish a layered rain-control strategy: site drainage and
  splash control, roofs and overflows, façade drainage planes, flashings, drained/ventilated cavities where used,
  opening sills/heads, penetration detailing, and safe discharge. Supporting source:
  [PHI planning resources](https://passipedia.org/planning).

  Caveat: airtightness, vapour control, and rain control are
  different functions even when one product participates in more than one layer.

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Evaluate surface and interstitial condensation,
  inward/outward vapour drives, solar vapour drive, air leakage, material moisture storage, and drying potential using a
  method proportionate to the assembly risk. PHI's construction-system criteria route surface-temperature analysis
  through ISO 10211, simplified interstitial-condensation assessment through ISO 13788, and higher-risk cases to dynamic
  EN 15026 analysis. Sources: [ISO 10211:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/65710.html) and
  [PHI construction-system criteria](https://passivehouse.com/media/filer_public/c9/17/c917b947-7ab8-4599-ad05-623c3e634040/03_certification_criteria_construction_systems_en.pdf).

  Caveat: a steady-state or simplified method does not replace transient hygrothermal analysis where moisture storage
  and changing climate are material.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Write a construction-moisture plan for weather
  protection, delivery/storage, wet-trade sequencing, temporary heat and dehumidification, substrate moisture testing,
  enclosure close-in criteria, leak response, and records. Supporting source:
  [PHI certification guidance](https://passipedia.org/certification/certified_passive_houses).

  Caveat: room air readings
  alone do not prove that timber, screed, insulation, or concealed substrates are dry enough.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: General principle.** Prefer details that remain inspectable, drainable, replaceable,
  and tolerant of foreseeable workmanship and equipment failure.

  Caveat: a calculation at handover does not establish
  long-term durability without compatible materials, execution quality, maintenance access, and moisture escape paths.

### Project evidence package

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Require an attributable project package for the
  household and the design team. It must contain climate and site inputs, energy model/version, assembly build-ups,
  material properties, junction calculations, window schedules and installation details, airtightness drawings and
  test reports, summer/moisture analyses, site photographs, deviations, and corrective actions. Supporting source:
  [PHI evidence-package guidance](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/).

  Caveat: a complete-looking
  folder is not evidence unless versions, authors, boundaries, and as-built correspondence are clear.

## Systems and operations

Researched/accessed 2026-08-10. Select systems from calculated loads, operating modes, maintainability, and failure
consequences. Do not infer performance from a product name or nominal rating.

### Ventilation

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: General principle.** Use balanced mechanical ventilation with heat recovery,
  or justified energy recovery, as an integrated duct-and-terminal system rather than treating the unit alone as the
  system. Source: [PHI building certification](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/).

  Caveat: the
  correct HRV/ERV choice depends on climate, moisture balance, occupancy, other exhaust systems, and the health targets
  owned by the healthy reference of this skill.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Coordinate intake/exhaust locations, outdoor
  contaminants, kitchen and wet-room strategy, transfer paths, pressure balance, frost protection, bypass, condensate
  drainage, duct insulation/airtightness, fire provisions, access, cleaning, controls, and failure modes. Supporting
  source: [PHI ventilation guides](https://passipedia.org/planning/guides_and_aids).

  Caveat: local code and the specific
  equipment instructions remain controlling. Romanian ventilation provisions route through `romanian-building-code`
  (see the ventilation records in `brief/systems.md`).

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Measure and balance delivered terminal flows in
  representative modes and record instrument, configuration, result, correction, and signed handover. Source:
  [ISO 16956:2015](https://www.iso.org/standard/58016.html) and
  [PHI v10c criteria](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download).

  Caveat: design flows or fan
  curves do not prove delivered room flow, pressure, noise, or heat-recovery performance.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** After the healthy reference of this skill supplies any
  health-specific filtration target, select fan pressure reserve, filter area, seals, access, and replacement alarms.

  Caveat: increasing
  filter efficiency without checking pressure drop, bypass, noise, controls, and maintenance can reduce ventilation.

### Heating, cooling, and DHW

- **Basis: Standard/certification. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Select a heat pump from room-by-room and
  whole-building design loads, source conditions, supply temperatures, DHW duty, defrost or ground-loop behavior,
  auxiliary heat, and part-load operation. Supporting source:
  [PHI heat-pump tool](https://passipedia.org/planning/heat_pump_tool).

  Caveat: nominal capacity and catalogue COP at
  one rating point do not establish seasonal performance or resilience.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Prefer the lowest distribution temperature that satisfies
  the verified room loads with controllable emitters and acceptable response. Supporting source:
  [PHI planning overview](https://passipedia.org/planning).

  Caveat: floor finishes, spacing, flow, balancing, thermal
  mass, setbacks, overheating, and hydraulic design can outweigh a generic “low temperature” preference.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** For radiant cooling, calculate sensible capacity by room,
  coordinate ventilation latent load, monitor dew point at representative locations, and provide condensation-safe
  supply-temperature limits, shutdown, alarms, and fallback cooling where required. Supporting source:
  [PHI v10c cooling/dehumidification criteria](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download).

  Caveat: radiant cooling does not remove moisture and surface condensation can damage finishes and assemblies.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Size DHW generation, storage, recirculation,
  distribution, and heat-loss control from actual occupancy and draw profiles. Coordinate hygiene, scald protection,
  recovery time, water quality, maintenance, and backup. Supporting source:
  [PHI planning overview](https://passipedia.org/planning).

  Caveat: a large buffer or storage tank can add standing
  loss, space, controls, and failure points. Health-specific water targets belong to the healthy reference of this
  skill.

### Renewables and grid

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Compare photovoltaic electricity, solar thermal, battery
  storage, and grid interaction on the same current load, yield, tariff, curtailment, export, replacement, maintenance,
  and resilience boundaries. Supporting source:
  [PHI v10c PER and renewable-generation method](https://univention.passiv.de/nextcloud/s/version10c-2023/download).

  Caveat: annual net balance can conceal seasonal mismatch, peak import, outage behavior, and changing Romanian tariff
  or prosumer rules.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Prioritize demand reduction and passive survivability
  before using additional generation or storage to mask avoidable loads. Supporting source:
  [PHI's five principles](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/).

  Caveat: the economic optimum still
  depends on project cost, roof/site constraints, embodied impacts, equipment life, incentives, and grid conditions.

### Controls, commissioning, and resilience

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Meter by decision boundary: whole-building import/export,
  renewable production, space conditioning, DHW, ventilation, and major auxiliaries as needed. Retain timestamped export
  and document meter accuracy and sign conventions. Supporting source:
  [PHI post-occupancy monitoring guidance](https://passipedia.org/planning/refurbishment_with_passive_house_components/post_occupancy_monitoring_performance_verification).

  Caveat: dashboards without stable boundaries, calibration, occupancy, and weather context do not prove performance.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: General principle.** Keep controls legible and operable without cloud services:
  label states, expose faults, retain safe manual overrides, document default/fallback modes, and avoid unnecessary app
  and gateway fragmentation.

  Caveat: automation cannot correct an undersized, unbalanced, inaccessible, or
  moisture-unsafe physical system.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Commission the integrated system across normal, peak,
  shoulder-season, DHW-priority, defrost/source-limit, high-humidity, outage, alarm, and manual modes. Record setpoints,
  measured flows/temperatures/power, observations, corrections, and ownership. Supporting source:
  [PHI certification process](https://passivehouse.com/en/home/building-certification/).

  Caveat: a successful individual
  equipment start-up is not integrated commissioning.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: Project-specific choice.** Provide filter and condensate access, isolation and
  drains, spares/consumables, service clearances, maintenance intervals, local service support, and an equipment-failure
  plan for heat, cooling, ventilation, and DHW.

  Caveat: low calculated load improves passive survivability but does not
  eliminate freeze, moisture, hygiene, or vulnerable-occupant risks during long failures.

### Durable lessons

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: General principle.** Preserve certification records, calculations, test reports,
  as-built details, commissioning data, and metered operations as separate evidence layers. Supporting sources:
  [PH Database 5581](https://passivehouse-database.org/index.php?lang=en#d_5581) and
  [Casa Veche series](https://casebune.ro/blogs/casa-veche).

  Caveat: later owner experience may reveal risks without
  changing what the historical certificate proved at its boundary.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: General principle.** Resolve structure, control layers, penetrations, and service
  routes before they become inaccessible. Test airtightness early and finally. Supporting sources:
  [roof-rework episode](https://casebune.ro/blogs/casa-veche/casaveche-ep-57-acoperis-ca-la-carte-fara-salaizan) and
  [airtightness series](https://casebune.ro/blogs/casa-veche/am-reusit-4-teste-si-2-ani-mai-tarziu-e-pasiva).

  Caveat:
  the appropriate sequence and tests depend on the actual assembly and responsible design team.

- **Basis: Design inference. Scope: General principle.** Design for construction drying, manual fallback, service
  access, equipment failure, and data export instead of optimizing only the calculated annual balance. Supporting
  sources:
  [winter drying](https://casebune.ro/blogs/casa-veche/casaveche-ep-61-nu-lasa-apa-in-sapa-si-pereti-mai-ales-iarna) and
  [heat-pump failure](https://casebune.ro/blogs/casa-buhnici/o-luna-fara-pompa-de-caldura-intr-o-casa-pasiva).

  Caveat:
  these case observations identify failure modes, not their probability or the correct project-specific remedy.
