# House brief conventions

The records in `brief/` are the semantic authority for the household's current accepted decisions. `budget/data/project.json` is the operational authority for budget categories, estimates, inclusion, ownership, completion, instalments, and payments. Budget classification never creates or replaces a household decision.

This template's rows are fictional examples and unaccepted until customized. They show how to record a decision without supplying a household programme, site, design, cost commitment, or compliance claim.

When a decision changes, reconcile the affected requirements, quantities, equipment, placement, and model geometry in the brief, budget, and maintained model sources. Save and reopen any changed model geometry. A price, inclusion, or accounting-state change alone does not change a requirement or require unrelated geometry work.

## Statuses

- `required`: preserve the accepted outcome until an explicit replacement decision.
- `preferred`: use as the accepted default and record material departures.
- `phased`: retain the accepted future intention without assuming initial delivery.
- `deferred`: leave the decision open until relevant evidence or a later decision.

## IDs and citations

Each decision table begins with `ID` and `Status`. Use the file prefixes `HH`, `SITE`, `LAY`, `RM`, `STY`, `HW`, `EN`, `TECH`, `GAR`, `AST`, and `EV`; add IDs only at the end and never reuse them. Cite the owning record rather than restating it, for example: [Energy, air, water, and resilience](energy-air-water-and-resilience.md) (`EN-01`).

Keep preferences distinct from inspiration, evidence, regulation, specialist guidance, and project-specific unknowns. Professionals must establish site conditions, design, cost, compliance, approvals, and construction information.
